# Consciousness guardrail

PivotPoint was partly motivated by questions about the apparent smallness of subjective control relative to the distributed brain. It is **not** currently a theory of consciousness.

The 2025 Cogitate adversarial collaboration is a useful methodological warning. It preregistered divergent predictions from Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT), tested them across fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG, and reported findings that both supported some predictions and substantially challenged key claims of both theories.

That matters here because PivotPoint can easily become a vocabulary that absorbs any neuroscience result after the fact.

Keep the levels separate:

```text
ENGINEERING QUESTION
Can a small local controller use unfinished work, dynamic routes and cheap interventions
better than a monolithic/global controller on held-out tasks?

NEUROSCIENCE QUESTION
Do biological systems contain analogous state-dependent control surfaces, routing gates
or re-entry mechanisms?

CONSCIOUSNESS QUESTION
Why/when does any of this accompany subjective experience?
```

Success at the first level does not answer the second or third. Similarity to a brain circuit does not make an engineering module conscious.

Any future consciousness claim needs predictions that distinguish PivotPoint from existing theories and are capable of failing on external data. Until then, keep consciousness as motivation and boundary, not benchmark result.
